# Downstream Devices → Semiconductor Output: a U.S. Industrial Lead-Factor Test

**Research date:** 19 October 2026 · **Status:** Executed macro-indicator falsification, **NOT** an equity-alpha backtest.

## Research question and mechanism

Does manufacturing activity in U.S. computers (NAICS 3341) and communications equipment (NAICS 3342) predict future U.S. semiconductor/electronic-component production (NAICS 3344) **beyond** semiconductor persistence and the broad manufacturing cycle? Rising downstream device production might signal chip orders, but inventories, international sourcing, vertical integration and a non-comparable geographic footprint can sever the transmission. WSTS's Spring 2026 industry outlook projects a large global AI/memory-driven expansion, but its 2026 forecast is **context only** and is never used as a historical predictor.

**H1:** downstream output growth minus semiconductor output growth contains incremental lead information. **H2:** an expanding-window factor improves 3-month-ahead YoY semiconductor production forecasts beyond a historical mean, semiconductor persistence and manufacturing/momentum controls. **H3:** any gain survives placebo, feature ablations, weight perturbations, release-lag sensitivity, horizon and regime tests.

## Data, factor and timing

Frozen monthly official Federal Reserve G.17 production-index snapshot, **2011-01 to 2026-09** (189 observations per series), retrieved **2026-10-19**, with G.17 last updated **2026-10-16**. Source-register rows document URL, release and vintage limitations. All index series are seasonally adjusted, 2017=100. No private data, estimated firm exposure weights, company fundamentals or stock returns are silently substituted.

At formation month **t**, every feature uses the source observation for **t−2** or earlier, a conservative *publication-lag assumption* (not an ALFRED real-time vintage). Let `g_j,s = 100*ln(IP_j,s / IP_j,s−12)` and define:

`gap_t = tanh(([w*g_computer,t−2 + (1−w)*g_communications,t−2] − g_semiconductor,t−2) / 15)` with `w=0.5` fixed in advance.

`y_t = g_semiconductor,t+3`. Train only on origins `u` whose label `u+3` would be observable by `t−2`, i.e. `u <= t−5`; fit a train-only standardized Ridge regression (α=10), never full-sample normalizers. Compare expanding-window mean, semiconductor persistence, semiconductor+manufacturing+3m momentum controls, and those controls plus the gap. Forecasts are for **future production YoY**, not securities returns.

## Findings (computed from checked-in data)

See `results/metrics.json` and `results/oos_predictions_minimal.csv` for checked-in core forecasts; `results/walk_forward_oos.csv` is generated by running the analysis for all diagnostics; the notebook shows calculated tables and 6 saved SVG charts. The initial expanding OOS window contains **108 monthly forecasts**. The gap does **not** improve the main controlled forecast: mean, persistence, macro and full RMSE are approximately **5.222, 4.468, 4.713 and 4.746 percentage points**, respectively. The six-month moving-block bootstrap 95% interval for RMSE(macro)−RMSE(full) spans zero. The pre-2022 fixed-fit block-permutation alignment test is **exploratory**, not an exact significance test for the adaptive walk-forward model.

**Verdict:** Strong global semiconductor demand narrative ≠ evidence of this specific U.S. production lead factor. The proposed gap fails to outperform the macro controls; there is **no evidence here of tradable equity alpha**. The revised-source snapshot, overlapping targets, 2017-base index rebasing, potential geography mismatch, absence of company exposures and return series are material limitations.
