## Purpose

Signals to Alpha tests whether observable shifts in consumption, production, construction and commodity markets anticipate measurable outcomes. The reference model is the [Consumption-to-Alpha K-Food study](Consumption-to-Alpha-A-U.S.-K-Food-Alternative-Data-Factor-Model/): explicit hypotheses, source provenance, fixed factor rules, independent outcomes, out-of-sample baselines, falsification and a frank verdict.

**Important distinction:** industrial-output forecasts, corporate fundamental associations and investable equity alpha are three different claims. A negative result is useful evidence. Fixed, revised data snapshots are not automatically point-in-time datasets.

## Research Summaries

### [Consumption-to-Alpha — U.S. K-Food Demand](Consumption-to-Alpha-A-U.S.-K-Food-Alternative-Data-Factor-Model/)

Annual K-Food demand and company category exposure show an economically plausible relationship with subsequent sales growth, but the equity signal is unstable. The five-year test's mean Rank IC is weak and the apparent return spread depends heavily on Samyang Foods. Exposure perturbations, company exclusions, regime splits and a within-year permutation placebo do not establish sector-wide investable alpha. **Verdict:** a useful demand-nowcasting hypothesis, not a validated trading strategy.

### [Grid-Capex-to-Alpha — U.S. Electrical Equipment](Grid-Capex-to-Alpha-U.S.-Electric-Equipment/)

Electricity demand and infrastructure investment provide a strong economic thesis, but a macro lead factor must still beat predictive baselines. The grid-output gap achieved six-month RMSE **4.124 pp** versus **4.228 pp** for momentum controls, yet lagged the historical-mean forecast (**3.524 pp**). An exploratory time-shift placebo (**p≈0.32**) did not support robust incremental value, and source vintages were not reconstructed. **Verdict:** useful structural context, but neither a verified point-in-time factor nor tested equity alpha.

### [Housing-Permits-to-Alpha — U.S. Building Materials](Housing-Permits-to-Alpha-U.S.-Building-Materials/)

Housing permits and starts are plausible precursors to construction-material demand but do not automatically forecast producer output. In **98** six-month-ahead expanding-window origins, housing-augmented Ridge had RMSE **5.109 pp**, worse than macro controls (**4.874 pp**) and a historical mean (**4.808 pp**). Alternative horizons, wood-products outcomes, regime splits and lag-only placebos did not establish consistent incremental value; historic vintages remain unaudited. **Verdict:** an informative negative macro-factor result, with no tested equity alpha.

### [Gas–Oil Divergence-to-Alpha — U.S. LNG](Gas-Oil-Divergence-to-Alpha-U.S.-LNG/)

Global LNG capacity expansion is a credible sector story, but relative oil–gas prices are not automatically a repeatable return signal. In **242** monthly expanding-window forecasts, the divergence model's RMSE was **32.707 pp** versus **30.911 pp** for momentum controls and **30.825 pp** for the historical mean. Alternative horizons, LNG-era splits, 2022 exclusion and stale-spread checks did not rescue the factor, and the data are not archival vintages. **Verdict:** failed natural-gas spot-price predictor; futures and equity alpha were not tested.

### [Device-Gap-to-Alpha — U.S. Semiconductors](Semiconductor-Device-Gap-to-Alpha-U.S.-Chip-Manufacturing/)

Downstream computer and communications-equipment output may lead chip production, but the global semiconductor demand story alone is not a predictive factor. In **108** expanding-window forecasts the device gap produced RMSE **4.746 pp**, worse than macro controls (**4.713 pp**) and semiconductor persistence (**4.468 pp**). A moving-block bootstrap interval straddles zero and an exploratory block-placebo (**p≈0.796**) offers no support; data are latest-revised snapshots. **Verdict:** no robust incremental production predictor and no tested equity alpha.

### [Charging-to-Adoption — Global EV Infrastructure](Charging-to-Adoption-Global-EV-Infrastructure/)

Across 16 selected national markets, 180 country-year observations from the IEA 2024 snapshot support 96 expanding-window EV sales-share forecasts for 2018–2023. Charging-augmented Ridge improves control-model RMSE only from **4.483** to **4.468 pp** and trails simple momentum at **4.416 pp**. A 1,000-refit within-year shuffle yields exploratory **p≈0.132** and a 2,000-draw year-block bootstrap interval **[−0.017, 0.052] pp** includes zero, with effects changing sign across regimes. **Verdict:** charging is a plausible infrastructure theme, but retrospective vintages, ex-post country selection and absent company returns prevent any validated point-in-time or equity-alpha claim.

### [E-Commerce Share-to-Alpha — U.S. Warehousing & Logistics](Ecommerce-Share-to-Alpha-U.S.-Warehousing-Logistics/)

E-commerce share acceleration has an economically plausible transmission channel into U.S. warehousing employment, but this is a revised-data retrospective study rather than a point-in-time trading strategy. Across 57 purged expanding-window two-quarter-ahead forecasts, the augmented model reduced RMSE to **5.172 pp**, compared with **5.869 pp** for payroll/persistence controls and **5.360 pp** for warehouse persistence alone. A 2,000-draw moving-block bootstrap interval for the macro-control improvement **[−1.486, 2.572] pp** crosses zero, while the 1,000-refit block-shuffle is exploratory and does not repair historical-vintage leakage. **Verdict:** a potentially useful macro lead-factor candidate, but historical release-vintage validity, corporate fundamentals and investable equity alpha remain unverified.

### [Home-Care Pressure-to-Alpha — U.S. Healthcare Services](Home-Care-Pressure-to-Alpha-U.S.-Healthcare-Services/)

Using 201 latest-revised BLS monthly observations and 99 purged expanding-window forecasts, the predeclared home-health-minus-hospital employment factor **worsened** six-month-ahead nursing/residential employment-growth RMSE to **5.562 pp**, versus **5.453 pp** for macro controls and **3.760 pp** for nursing persistence. A 1,000-refit 12-month block shuffle produced an exploratory tail fraction of **0.730**, while the 2,000-draw moving-block bootstrap 95% interval for macro-minus-full RMSE was **[-0.268, -0.017] pp**. Alternative horizons, release buffers and pre/post-2020 regimes did not rescue the base factor; the 1,000-draw segment-exposure mixture was sensitive to how overlapping ambulatory/home-care series were weighted. **Verdict:** reproducible negative macro lead-factor pilot only; retrospective CES revisions, unknown historical release vintages, absent corporate fundamentals and no securities returns prevent any point-in-time or investable-alpha claim.

### [Air-Travel-to-Alpha — U.S. Accommodation & Hospitality](Air-Travel-to-Alpha-U.S.-Accommodation-Hospitality/)

U.S. BTS air revenue passenger miles and BLS lodging/restaurant payrolls form a retrospective 2014–2026 fixed-snapshot panel with **82** purged expanding-window three-month-ahead forecasts. The air–restaurant gap improved bounded-control Ridge RMSE from **46.313** to **35.015 pp**, but failed the historical-mean baseline at **22.937 pp**. A 2,000-draw moving-block bootstrap interval for control-minus-full RMSE **[−1.963, 23.111] pp** spans zero, and excluding 2020 reverses the improvement. **Verdict:** a pandemic-sensitive negative macro lead-factor pilot with unaudited historical publication vintages, manually transcribed source rows and no firm-level or equity-return alpha.

### [Basic-Chemical-to-Alpha — U.S. Pharmaceutical Manufacturing](Basic-Chemical-to-Alpha-U.S.-Pharmaceutical-Manufacturing/)

Basic-chemical manufacturing output relative to aggregate manufacturing is an economically plausible upstream indicator for U.S. pharmaceutical production, but the 2026-10-26 revised G.17 snapshot is not a point-in-time vintage panel. Across **111** purged expanding-window six-month forecasts, the augmented model **worsened** RMSE to **4.104 pp**, versus **3.953 pp** for pharmaceutical-persistence and manufacturing controls and **4.347 pp** for the historical mean. A 1,000-refit 12-month block-shuffle placebo produced an exploratory tail fraction **0.925**, while the 2,000-draw moving-block bootstrap improvement interval **[−0.504, 0.189] pp** includes zero. **Verdict:** a negative retrospective macro-factor falsification, with unknown historical publication vintages, no firm fundamentals and no investable equity-alpha evidence.

### [Textile-Mill Gap-to-Alpha — U.S. Apparel & Leather](Textile-Mill-Gap-to-Alpha-U.S.-Apparel-Leather/)

Textile-and-product-mill production growth relative to manufacturing was tested against subsequent U.S. apparel-and-leather output using 201 latest-revised monthly observations and 111 purged expanding-window forecasts. The textile-augmented model worsened RMSE to **9.507 pp** versus **9.280 pp** for macro controls and **8.568 pp** for the historical mean. A 1,000-refit exploratory block shuffle yielded a **0.855** tail fraction, and a 2,000-draw paired moving-block bootstrap gave **[−0.490, −0.046] pp** for control-minus-full RMSE. **Verdict:** a negative retrospective macro-factor pilot with historical publication vintages unverified, no issuer fundamentals and no investable equity-return evidence.

### [Tourism-to-Foodservice — U.S. Restaurants](Tourism-to-Foodservice-U.S.-Restaurant-Macro-Leading-Indicator/)

Using a previously frozen BTS/BLS 2014–2026 panel, 70 purged expanding-window origins forecast three-month-ahead U.S. foodservice employment growth; this is a distinct NAICS 722 outcome, not an independent new data source. The tourism-gap-augmented Ridge worsened RMSE to **71.659 pp** from **66.047 pp** for lodging/momentum controls and **15.186 pp** for the historical mean. A 1,000-refit block-shuffle tail fraction of **0.896**, a 2,000-draw paired block-bootstrap interval **[−9.725, −0.115] pp**, and zero positive gains in 1,000 exposure perturbations fail to support incremental predictive value. **Verdict:** negative retrospective macro-factor falsification; inherited manual transcription, missing historical vintages and absent issuer fundamentals/returns preclude PIT or equity-alpha claims.

### [Paper-Input Gap — U.S. Printing & Related Support](Paper-Input-Gap-to-Alpha-U.S.-Printing-Manufacturing/)

Using 201 manually transcribed official Federal Reserve G.17 monthly observations (2010–2026), 114 purged expanding-window origins tested whether the paper–manufacturing gap leads U.S. printing output growth three months ahead. The paper-augmented Ridge model worsened OOS RMSE to **6.366 log pp** versus **6.184** for printing/manufacturing controls and **6.326** for the historical mean. A 1,000-refit block-aware placebo gave an exploratory upper-tail fraction **0.987**, while a 2,000-draw paired block-bootstrap 95% interval **[−0.657, 0.085] log pp** spans zero; alternate horizons, pandemic exclusions and 200 paper-weight perturbations did not rescue the factor. **Verdict:** a negative retrospective macro-factor falsification based on a manually transcribed 2026-10-16 revised snapshot, without verified historical release vintages, issuer fundamentals or investable equity returns.

### [Air-Traffic Gap — U.S. Aerospace Manufacturing](Air-Traffic-Gap-to-Alpha-U.S.-Aerospace-Manufacturing/)

Using 201 revised-snapshot monthly aerospace/manufacturing observations and 150 airline observations, 77 purged expanding-window origins tested a fixed air-traffic-minus-manufacturing gap against six-month-ahead aerospace production growth. The augmented Ridge worsened OOS RMSE to **19.890 pp** from **16.967 pp** for macro controls and **13.810 pp** for the historical mean. A 1,000-refit 12-month block shuffle produced exploratory tail fraction **0.932**, while a 2,000-draw paired circular-block bootstrap interval for control-minus-full RMSE was **[−6.511, −0.369] pp** and no ±20% exposure perturbations improved the control. **Verdict:** negative retrospective aerospace macro-factor falsification; unverified historical publication vintages, reused manually transcribed inputs, and absent issuer fundamentals/total returns preclude PIT or investable equity-alpha claims.

### [Energy-Feedstock Gap — U.S. Basic Chemical Manufacturing](Energy-Feedstock-Gap-to-Alpha-U.S.-Basic-Chemicals/)

Using 201 fixed/revised G.17/EIA monthly observations and 111 purged expanding-window origins, the two-month-stale gas–oil inflation gap failed to improve six-month-ahead U.S. basic-chemicals production forecasts. The augmented Ridge returned **9.034 log-pp RMSE** versus **8.971** for manufacturing/oil controls and **8.271** for the historical mean. A 1,000-refit 12-month block shuffle gave an exploratory tail fraction **0.521**, and the 2,000-draw paired block-bootstrap improvement interval **[−0.373, 0.219] log pp** includes zero; all 1,000 exposure perturbations remained negative. **Verdict:** negative retrospective macro-factor falsification, with unreconstructed historical publication vintages, no issuer fundamentals and no security returns.

### [Printing-Demand Gap — U.S. Paper Manufacturing](Printing-Demand-Gap-to-Alpha-U.S.-Paper-Manufacturing/)

Using 201 inherited revised G.17 monthly observations and 120 purged expanding-window origins, the two-month-stale printing-minus-manufacturing gap reduced three-month-ahead paper-production RMSE to **4.175 log-pp** from **4.448** for macro controls, **4.291** for persistence and **4.559** for the historical mean. A 1,000-refit 12-month block shuffle yielded an exploratory upper-tail fraction **0.01499**, while the 2,000-draw paired block-bootstrap improvement interval was **[0.033, 0.570] log-pp** and all 1,000 proxy-weight perturbations retained a positive gain. However, the pre-2020 gain was negative (**−0.107 log-pp**), 12-month-horizon gains reversed sign, and the unconditional quintile growth spread was negative; only 10 of 603 inherited source values were independently spot-checked. **Verdict:** conditional retrospective macro lead-factor candidate, **not PIT**, with no issuer fundamentals, securities returns or investable equity-alpha claim.

### [Warehouse Capacity → Online Retail Share — U.S. Retail Trade](Warehouse-Capacity-to-Retail-Share-U.S.-Online-Retail/)

Using 107 frozen quarterly Census/BLS observations (2000–2026), 57 purged expanding-window origins tested whether a one-quarter-stale warehouse hiring premium predicts the next two-quarter change in U.S. e-commerce retail share. The factor improved control-model RMSE from **1.3233 to 1.2803 pp**, but lagged the historical mean at **0.8223 pp** and worsened MAE. A 1,000-refit four-quarter block shuffle yielded exploratory tail fraction **0.05994**, while a 2,000-draw paired block-bootstrap 95% interval **[−0.0423, 0.1080] pp** crossed zero and excluding 2020–2021 reversed the improvement. **Verdict:** negative/unstable retrospective retail macro lead-factor pilot; inherited revised data, missing historical vintages and absent firm-level returns preclude PIT or investable equity-alpha claims.

### [Relative Copper–Aluminum Cost Relief — Historical World Copper Demand](Relative-Copper-Aluminum-Cost-Relief-Global-Copper-Demand/)

Using the 25-year 1951–1975 statsmodels/Gill world-copper panel, 10 purged expanding-window annual origins tested whether stale relative copper/aluminum price growth predicts next-year copper consumption. The augmented model achieved **6.290 log-pp** OOS RMSE versus **6.297** for demand/income/inventory controls and **7.625** for the historical mean, an economically tiny **0.0068 log-pp** control-relative improvement. A 1,000-refit exploratory three-year-block shuffle gave tail fraction **0.273**, while a 2,000-draw block-bootstrap 95% interval **[-0.0040, 0.0155] log-pp** crosses zero and 2–3-year horizons fail. **Verdict:** a negative/indeterminate retrospective industrial-metal demand pilot, not historical PIT or investable equity alpha; original release vintages, firm fundamentals and total returns are absent.

### [Manufacturing Catch-up Gap — U.S. Motor Vehicles & Parts](Manufacturing-Catchup-Gap-to-Auto-Production-U.S.-Motor-Vehicles/)

The two-month-stale nonlinear manufacturing–auto momentum gap was tested against three-month-ahead U.S. motor-vehicle-and-parts production growth using 120 purged expanding-window OOS forecasts from the revised 2026-10-16 G.17 snapshot. Adding the factor worsened RMSE from 28.532 to 29.526 log-pp, and the historical-mean baseline achieved 24.621 log-pp. A 1,000-refit 12-month block-reorder placebo yielded an exploratory tail fraction 0.987, while the 2,000-draw paired block-bootstrap interval [-1.868, -0.014] log-pp and pandemic-exclusion checks did not rescue the signal. **Verdict:** a reproducible negative retrospective macro-production pilot, with manually transcribed and incompletely audited rows, no historical PIT vintages, no company fundamentals and no tested equity total-return alpha.

### [Online-Order Pressure — U.S. Couriers & Messengers](Online-Order-Pressure-to-Courier-Employment-U.S.-Parcel-Delivery/)

Using 107 inherited revised quarterly Census/BLS observations, 61 purged expanding-window forecasts tested whether lagged online-order pressure anticipates U.S. couriers-and-messengers employment growth two quarters ahead. The augmented model reduced OOS RMSE to **4.629 log-pp** from **5.148** for payroll controls but barely exceeded simple courier persistence (**4.639**). A 1,000-refit four-quarter block placebo produced exploratory tail fraction **0.0010**, while the 2,000-draw paired block-bootstrap interval **[−0.390, +1.388] log-pp** crossed zero and pre/post-pandemic gains were negative. **Verdict:** pandemic-dependent retrospective macro-factor falsification, with unreconstructed historical vintages, reused quarterly aggregates and no issuer fundamentals, total returns or investable equity-alpha evidence.

### [Inflation–Labor Pressure — U.S. Treasury Money Markets](Inflation-Labor-Pressure-to-TBill-Yields-U.S.-Money-Markets/)

Using the frozen 2009 FRED/BLS compilation for 1959Q1–2009Q3, 115 purged expanding-window forecasts tested whether a one-quarter-stale inflation–unemployment pressure score predicts next-quarter changes in the 3-month Treasury bill rate. The factor-augmented model improved RMSE from **0.8173** to **0.8080 percentage points** relative to the macro control, but failed the historical mean (**0.7381**) and zero-change (**0.7229**) baselines. A 1,000-refit block shuffle produced an exploratory upper-tail fraction **0.0579**, while a 2,000-draw paired block-bootstrap interval **[−0.0355, 0.0497] pp** included zero. **Verdict:** retrospective money-market rate-prediction pilot only; historical release vintages, actual Treasury bill total returns, issuer fundamentals and trading costs were not reconstructed, so neither PIT predictability nor investable alpha is established.

## Common Research Format

Every study README follows the same **15 sections**: Abstract; Research Design; Industry Context; Data & Source Integrity; Point-in-Time Availability; Factor Construction; Exposure & Transmission; Outcomes & Baselines; Empirical Findings; Robustness & Placebos; Regime Sensitivity; Bias Audit & Verdict; Reproduction; Next-Stage Protocol; Sources & Artifacts.

Each study also has `study.json` with a notebook path, source register, artifacts, tests and honest evidence level. Previously published detailed source notes remain in `DETAILS.md` where applicable. Existing executed notebooks and numeric data have **not** been rewritten merely for consistent formatting.

The [research quality standard](RESEARCH_QUALITY_STANDARD.md) and [new-study template](STUDY_TEMPLATE.md) define the publication gate. Validate documentation integrity locally:

```bash
python scripts/validate_studies.py
```

## Reproducing a Study

1. Open the individual study README and inspect its data source register, vintage caveat and analysis target.
2. Install the listed dependencies, use the study-specific reproduction command, and run its tests.
3. Inspect the notebook, original data files and recorded results. Cross-check any newly computed numbers against the published snapshot before citing them.
4. Treat archived, mixed-vintage forecasts as retrospective evidence only until original publication vintages are verified.

**All published results are research-only and not investment advice.** No currently indexed study establishes a fully point-in-time, transaction-cost-aware equity-alpha strategy.

## Contributing a Study

Follow [STUDY_TEMPLATE.md](STUDY_TEMPLATE.md); add an industry directory with `README.md`, `study.json`, a stand-alone executed notebook, primary-source register, frozen inputs, reproducible analysis code, tests, and computed figures/results. Write a 3–4-sentence summary into this index and identify negative findings without dilution. A new run is accepted only after evidence and execution gates pass; otherwise keep it as a draft and report the blocker.

Automated deep-research batches are managed separately from GitHub Actions. The repository's Actions check research layout and existing executable notebooks; the CI workflow does **not** autonomously generate new industry studies.

### [Fuel-Cost Relief — U.S. Airline Transportation](Fuel-Cost-Relief-to-Airline-Demand-U.S.-Air-Transportation/)

WTI crude-oil cost relief was tested against three-month-ahead U.S. airline passenger-mile growth using **93** release-purged expanding-window origins on inherited 2014–2026 BTS/BLS/EIA snapshots. The oil-augmented model worsened RMSE to **107.776 log-pp** from **107.082** for airline/lodging/foodservice controls and **94.303** for the historical mean. A 1,000-refit year-block shuffle gave an exploratory tail fraction **0.615**, while a 2,000-draw paired block-bootstrap interval **[−2.032, 2.304] log-pp** included zero and none of 1,000 oil-horizon-weight perturbations improved the controls. **Verdict:** negative retrospective U.S. airline macro-factor pilot; historical publication vintages, issuer-level fundamentals and total returns remain unverified, so no PIT or investable equity-alpha claim.

### [Market Valuation → Capex — Historical U.S. Industrial Firms](Market-Valuation-to-Capex-U.S.-Industrial-Firms/)

Using 220 historical Grunfeld firm-years across 11 selected American industrial firms, 110 purged expanding-window predictions for 1945–1954 test whether a lagged market-value-to-capital proxy forecasts real gross investment. The augmented model reduces log-investment RMSE from **0.2929** to **0.2616** versus capital/momentum controls, but fails to beat simple investment persistence at **0.2544**. A 1,000-refit within-firm block shuffle gives an exploratory upper-tail fraction **0.0030**, and the 2,000-draw paired year-block bootstrap improvement interval is **[0.0112, 0.0488]**, while a documented US Steel 1946 source-version discrepancy and the selected postwar panel limit external validity. **Verdict:** a retrospective firm-fundamentals lead candidate only, with unreconstructed historical publication vintages, no representative industrial universe and no investable equity-return evidence.
