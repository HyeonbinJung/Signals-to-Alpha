- The 189-row fixed G.17 CSV is copied byte-for-byte from the repository's original semiconductor study, verified against Git blob SHA `3d111120b93de0a5eb153d794772225f5dd1a6c4` on 2026-10-28. Local SHA-256 is `6a6fca4b29755f0c2cae8ccfae1016a22648e724787cc42e726ac945583c351c`. No independent complete-series retrieval or historical first-release vintage audit occurred.
- The Federal Reserve G.17 September 2026 release is dated **2026-10-16**. The 2011–2026 historical rows are latest-revised and are not necessarily the values known in their original months. For each formation month t the model uses t-2 data, but this only approximates normal publication lag and **does not** solve historical revision leakage. Exact first-release timestamps and revision sizes are **UNKNOWN**.
- Monthly index values are positive, complete, date-unique and contiguous; 2017=100 seasonally adjusted. The 2026-09 source values were not independently re-downloaded. The upstream original Git blob identity is the only byte-level independent cross-check.
- The outcome is 3-month-ahead **computer-hardware industry production YoY**, not company sales or tradable returns. The NAICS 3341 industry is geographically U.S. production, whereas Dell/HP global filings do not provide a backtestable historical NAICS exposure panel. Issuer rows are narrative only; leave-one-company-out and issuer IC/return spreads are **NOT APPLICABLE**, not silently claimed as passed.
- OOS origins require >=48 historical fully labeled examples; training u+h<=t-2, h=3, and train-only standardization. Fixed Ridge alpha=20, no hyperparameter search. Overlapping YoY labels and 12-month moving-block resampling make uncertainty exploratory. Reordering 12-month factor blocks is a **diagnostic** null, not a formal exchangeability-based permutation p-value.
- Publication/vintage, source reuse, correlated industry output, time series nonstationarity, COVID regime, multiple-hypothesis exploration, omitted capacity/price/shipments, data selection and absence of securities universe/fees/costs are material biases. Industry-level factor failure does not establish absence of a corporate signal.
